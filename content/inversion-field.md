## An inside-out field

Inversion Field is an EigenDynamics experiment in prismatic tunnels, repeated structures, color, and continuous geometric movement.

Rather than representing an everyday physical place, the work constructs a space from transformation and perspective. Its identity comes from what happens as the viewer moves through the field.

## Visual questions

- When does repetition read as depth rather than as ornament?
- How can a changing perspective make a stable form appear to transform?
- Which camera trajectories keep a distorted world legible?
- How do color and geometric density affect the impression of motion?

The illustration here is diagrammatic; it is not a screenshot or a faithful reproduction of the original color palette.

## The larger inquiry

A technically interesting transformation is not automatically a compelling animation. The work involves both mathematical structure and the artistic problem of selecting which structures to reveal.

See [Spiral Julia](entry.html?slug=spiral-julia) and [From Image to Cell](entry.html?slug=from-image-to-cell) for different approaches to transformation and structure.

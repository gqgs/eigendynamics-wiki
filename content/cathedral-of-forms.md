## Recursive architecture as a world

Cathedral of Forms is an experiment in luminous geometric architecture: recursive structures, suspended forms, and a slow journey through a space that feels architectural without simply reproducing familiar buildings.

The project examines how mathematical composition can suggest scale, atmosphere, and place.

## The questions

- How much repetition is necessary for geometry to feel architectural?
- What happens when a structure is familiar at one scale and strange at another?
- Can a moving viewpoint communicate a spatial rule better than a still image?
- Which forms should remain stable while the environment becomes more elaborate?

A navigable world and a successful standalone image are not the same artifact. Their visual and technical constraints often diverge.

## Connection to EigenDynamics

The study connects procedural geometry to the broader ambition of building explorable mathematical worlds. It sits beside [Inversion Field](entry.html?slug=inversion-field), which puts a stronger emphasis on transformation and perspective.
